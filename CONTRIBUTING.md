# Contributing

Bring something worth opening, understanding and keeping.

## What belongs

Resources should fit weird hardware, low-level systems, operating systems,
reverse engineering, embedded Linux, firmware, networking, retro computing,
hardware hacking or technically unusual experiments. An entry should teach
something, solve a real problem, document hardware or preserve a clever
implementation. Popularity and star counts are not admission criteria.

## Suggesting an entry

- Link to the original project, specification, documentation or author's writeup.
- Check that the destination works and contains the promised technical material.
- Write one concise sentence explaining what it is and why it belongs.
- Put it in the most useful existing section and check for duplicate projects,
  alternate domains and mirrors first.
- Label prototypes, historical resources, abandoned implementations and
  simulations honestly; do not turn an experiment into a production claim.
- Explain in the issue or pull request what you inspected and what a reader can learn.

A project and a separate article belong together only if the article adds
substantial technical evidence or explanation. Self-promotion is welcome under
exactly the same quality bar; promotional spam, affiliate lists and link dumps
are not. Please suggest small, reviewable additions instead of bulk imports.

## Corrections and maintenance

Report broken links, misleading descriptions, changed project names and better
primary sources. A historical resource may be retained when its technical value
survives, with its status made explicit. A successor should replace an obsolete
tool unless the older implementation teaches something distinct.

Text contributions are accepted under the repository's MIT license. Images need
clear ownership or reuse permission, useful alt text, a local file and attribution
in `assets/README.md`. Linking to an external project does not license its content
for copying into this repository.
